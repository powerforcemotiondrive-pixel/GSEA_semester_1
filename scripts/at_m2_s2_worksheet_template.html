Case Study Overview: Modern Airliner Flight Controls
Commercial transport aircraft (such as the Airbus A320 and Boeing 777) utilize Fly-By-Wire (FBW) systems to translate pilot stick inputs into digital actuator commands. Primary control surfaces (ailerons, elevators, rudder) deflect to control the aircraft about its three axes, powered by triple-redundant hydraulic circuits.
System Actuation Power Operational Function
Primary Controls Triple Hydraulic Redundancy (Green/Blue/Yellow) Continuous attitude control (Roll, Pitch, Yaw) via ailerons, elevators, rudder.
Secondary Controls Hydraulic Power Units + Screwjack Motors High-lift augmentation (flaps, slats) and lift-dumping/speed braking (spoilers).
Emergency Backup Ram Air Turbine (RAT) + Accumulators Deploys automatically if all engine generators and hydraulic pumps fail.
Key Systems Takeaway: Inboard vs. Outboard Ailerons: At high cruise speeds (above 250 kt), outboard ailerons are locked out to prevent aerodynamic wing twisting (aeroelastic torsion); roll is commanded solely by inboard ailerons and roll spoilers.

Part 5: Technical Error Hunter: Spotting Aircraft Anatomy Mistakes

Partner Reading & Error Identification: Challenging Fallacies with Precise Technical Corrections
Activity 5 Protocol & Active Listening Rubric Partner Interaction
Partner Protocol: Student A reads Statement 1 aloud. Student B listens actively (without looking at the text), identifies the technical error, explains *why* it is aerodynamically or mechanically wrong, and states the accurate correction. Then swap roles for each statement!
Statement 01: Ailerons & Lift

"The aileron is there to produce lift during takeoff and initial climb."

Correction: Ailerons control roll by deflecting differentially (one up, one down); flaps and slats increase lift.
Statement 02: Pitot Tube Function

"Pitot tubes measure static atmospheric pressure to calculate altitude."

Correction: Pitot tubes measure dynamic ram air pressure for airspeed; static ports measure altitude.
Statement 03: APU Operational Role

"The APU in the tail cone provides supplementary thrust during climb."

Correction: The APU generates electrical power and pneumatic bleed air; it produces zero propulsive thrust.
Statement 04: Leading-Edge Slats

"Leading-edge slats extend from the trailing edge to increase drag."

Correction: Slats extend from the leading edge to delay stall; flaps extend from the trailing edge.
Statement 05: Rudder in Cruise Flight

"Pilots use the rudder pedals to steer and turn the plane during cruise."

Correction: Turns in flight are made by banking with ailerons; the rudder is used only to coordinate turns.
Statement 06: Static Discharge Wicks

"Static discharge wicks are thick cables connected to fuel tankers."

Correction: Static wicks bleed electrical charges built up from air friction into the atmosphere during flight.
Statement 07: Ground Spoilers Deployment

"Ground spoilers deploy during cruise to maintain top cruise speed."

Correction: Ground spoilers deploy on touchdown to dump lift and transfer weight to wheel brakes.
Statement 08: Radome Material Composition

"The radome is made of thick titanium to protect against bird strikes."

Correction: Radomes must be dielectric composite (fiberglass/quartz); metal would block radar radio waves.
