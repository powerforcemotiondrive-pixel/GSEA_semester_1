📌 Lesson Summary & Spoken English Context

In this 2-hour communicative session, students examine the international crisis of mass tourism across Europe and global fragile ecosystems. 📰 City Tourism Headlines in the International Press

Headline A: Venice Expands €5 Day-Tripper Access Ticket

Venice municipal council voted to double the pilot €5 entry fee to €10 on peak holiday weekends. City officials claim the levy deters short-stay crowds, but resident associations protest that it transforms their living city into a commercial theme park.

Speaking Prompt: Can an entry ticket genuinely manage city crowding?
Headline B: Amsterdam Freezes Central Hotel Construction

Amsterdam has banned all new hotel development and capped annual river cruise dockings. The city plans to relocate its sea cruise terminal outside the historic centre by 2035 to protect residential neighborhoods from street nuisance and overcrowding.

Speaking Prompt: Why are European capitals switching from promotion to containment?

🧠 Session 2 Vocabulary Retrieval Quick Check

Concept 01: Modal Shift Transport Mode

Partner Question: What operational change does a modal shift represent?

Verification: A switch by passengers or freight from carbon-intensive modes (private cars, short-haul flights) to sustainable rail and transit.
Concept 02: Decarbonization Emissions Cut

Partner Question: How does decarbonization differ from carbon offsetting?

Verification: Eliminating greenhouse emissions directly at the source through fleet electrification and SAF, rather than paying for external offsets.
Concept 03: Externalities Social Costs

Partner Question: Why aren't externalities reflected in commercial travel prices?

Verification: Unintended side-effects (noise, nitrogen emissions, road wear) paid by society and taxpayers rather than private operators.
Concept 04: Lifecycle Assessment (LCA) Methodology

Partner Question: What scope does a full lifecycle assessment cover?

Verification: Cradle-to-grave analysis evaluating environmental impacts from raw material extraction, through manufacturing and operation, to recycling.

01. Venice, Italy

49,000 residents
Annual Volume: 25+ Million Visitors (Ratio ~ 510:1)

Social Impacts: Severe residential depopulation (down from 175,000 in 1950), loss of neighborhood grocery stores replaced by souvenir stalls, extreme waterbus crowding.

Municipal Measures: Implemented €5 peak-day entrance fee for day-trippers; banned large cruise liners over 25,000 gross tonnes from the historic Giudecca Canal.

02. Dubrovnik, Croatia

41,000 residents
Annual Volume: 1.5 Million Visitors (Walled city: <1,000 residents)

Social Impacts: "Game of Thrones" filming triggered massive crowd bottlenecks at the medieval Pile Gate; local services completely priced out by seasonal tourism rentals.

Municipal Measures: "Respect the City" programme: capped cruise ship arrivals to a maximum of 4,000 disembarking passengers simultaneously; installed AI crowd tracking cameras.

03. Amsterdam, Netherlands

880,000 residents
Annual Volume: 20 Million Visitors

Social Impacts: Nuisance nightlife tourism in the central canal ring; proliferation of short-term holiday rentals drove rent prices up 65% in 8 years.

Municipal Measures: "Stay Away" demographic advertising campaigns; 30-night annual cap on short-term rentals; ban on new central souvenir shops and new hotel construction.

04. Maya Bay, Thailand

Protected National Park
Peak Influx: 5,000 Visitors / Day on a 250m Beach

Social Impacts: Boat propellers and toxic chemical sunscreens destroyed over 80% of native coral reefs; blacktip reef shark populations abandoned their natural nursery.

Municipal Measures: Complete 3.5-year closure for ecological regeneration; reopened with a strict 375-visitor hourly cap, mandatory boardwalks, and a boat ban inside the bay.

🗣️ Spoken Comparative Framework & Sentence Connectors

Expressing Contrast & Severity:
  • "While Amsterdam struggles with nightlife nuisance, Venice faces..."
  • "Maya Bay's crisis is purely ecological, whereas Dubrovnik's is..."
  • "The visitor-to-inhabitant ratio in Venice is dramatically higher than..."
Evaluating Policy Effectiveness:
  • "An entry fee merely monetizes crowding rather than preventing it."
  • "A complete seasonal closure proved far more effective because..."
  • "Without hard legal caps on accommodation, day levies cannot..."

Policy Option A

Short-Term Rental Quota (Airbnb Cap)

Limit residential holiday rentals to a maximum of 30 nights per calendar year per primary residence. Require verified digital municipal licenses to protect housing affordability for nurses, students, and teachers.

Chamber of Commerce Objection: Infringes on private property ownership and deprives local property owners of supplemental income during inflation.
Policy Option B

€10 Daily Peak-Season Entry Levy

Levy a €10 daily access charge on all non-resident day-trippers entering the historic perimeter during weekends and bank holidays, collected via regional train tickets and parking terminals.

Mega-Cruise Ship Port Moratorium

Prohibit passenger vessels exceeding 50,000 gross tonnes from berthing within 10 km of the historic harbour to eliminate heavy bunker fuel emissions and sudden 5,000-passenger crowd surges.

Chamber of Commerce Objection: Port authority and provisioning logistics operators lose millions in maritime docking tariffs and vessel refuelling contracts.

Part 4: Grammar Focus — Causatives of Authority & Contrast Connectors

Student Instructions & Action Steps:
  1. Step 1 (Rule Review): Study the 3 core grammar formulas below for expressing municipal rules and balancing competing viewpoints.
  2. Step 2 (Diagnostic Quiz): Complete the 4 TOEIC-style test questions individually without consulting notes.
  3. Step 3 (Oral Transformation): With your partner, create 2 original sentences applying in spite of and make + bare infinitive to your city policy pitch.
Structure 1

Causatives of Authority

Make + obj + bare V1
Require + obj + to-V1
Have + obj + done

Example: The city council made tour companies limit walking groups to 20 people.
Example: Officials required hotels to install low-flow water taps.

Structure 2

Contrast Prepositions

Despite + noun / gerund
In spite of + noun / gerund
❌ Never: *despite of

Example: Despite higher tourist taxes, hotel occupancy reached 94%.
Example: In spite of banning cruise ships, retail sales remained steady.

Structure 3

Concession Clauses

Clause 1, whereas + Clause 2
While + Clause 1, Clause 2

Example: Hoteliers celebrated peak summer revenue, whereas local residents struggled with nighttime noise.
Example: While day-trippers spend little, they consume public services.

📝 Interactive Grammar Challenge (TOEIC Parts 5 & 6 Traps)

1. __________ the daily visitor cap, historic canal bridges remained congested during the holiday.

(A) In spite of    (B) Although    (C) Because of    (D) Whereas
✓ Key: (A) — Followed by noun phrase ("the daily visitor cap"). (B) requires subject + verb.

2. The municipal council __________ apartment owners register holiday rentals on an official database.

(A) made    (B) required    (C) let    (D) had
✓ Key: (B) — Follows pattern "require + object + to-infinitive". "Make" takes bare infinitive.

3. Overnight guests support cultural museums, __________ day-trippers rarely purchase museum tickets.

(A) despite    (B) in spite    (C) whereas    (D) because
✓ Key: (C) — "Whereas" links two complete contrasting clauses with balanced syntax.

4. Port authorities __________ cruise operators reschedule arrivals to avoid simultaneous docking surges.

(A) caused    (B) forced to    (C) made    (D) allowed
✓ Key: (C) — "Made + operators + reschedule" uses causative of authority without "to".

🚨 Emergency Crisis Dossier: Isla Serena Summer Drought

Aquifer Shortage: 18% Capacity

Situation: Isla Serena (Mediterranean island; permanent population: 24,000) experiences an August influx of 120,000 tourists. A 14-month drought has collapsed municipal reservoirs. Luxury coastal hotel resorts consume 800 liters of water per guest per day for private pools and tropical gardens, while local resident households face water cuts capped at 90 liters per person per day. Simultaneously, airport operators have proposed expanding the runway by 600 meters onto agricultural citrus groves to welcome wide-body long-haul jets.

Aquifer Level 18% (Critical)
Resort Consumption 800 L / guest / day
Resident Quota 90 L / resident / day
Runway Proposal +600m on Farmland
Role A: The Town Mayor (Chair)

Must preserve municipal tax revenue and seasonal employment, but cannot allow residential taps to run dry. Needs a politically defensible compromise before August municipal elections.

Role B: The Hotel Association President

Represents 45 luxury hotels employing 1,500 local staff. Insists high-paying guests demand daily pools and lawns. Strongly supports runway expansion to attract direct premium flights.

Role C: The Organic Citrus Farmer

Produces olive oil and citrus. Demands agricultural water quotas to prevent tree death. Completely opposes runway expansion because it concretes fertile heritage agricultural land.

Role D: Environmental Protection Regulator

Warns that further aquifer pumping risks irreversible saltwater intrusion that will poison the island's groundwater for decades. Demands hard tourism caps and an immediate runway veto.

📚 Master Glossary: 18 Key Overtourism Terms

Term & Pronunciation Operational Definition Term & Pronunciation Operational Definition
Overtourism /ˌoʊ.vəˈtʊə.rɪ.zəm/ Excessive visitor volume causing social and ecological degradation. Visitor Dispersal /dɪˈspɜː.səl/ Directing tourist crowds away from crowded centres to regional towns.
Carrying Capacity /kəˈpæs.ə.ti/ Maximum visitors a site sustains without damaging local culture or nature. Honey-Pot Destination /ˈhʌn.i pɒt/ Hyper-popular iconic site drawing disproportionate visitor densities.
De-Marketing /diːˈmɑː.kɪ.tɪŋ/ Advertising strategies designed to discourage tourist arrivals. Infrastructure Strain /ˈɪn.frəˌstrʌk.tʃər/ Overloading sewage, water, waste disposal, and transport capacity.

Resident Backlash /ˈbæk.læʃ/ Local protests against housing displacement and noise nuisance. Seasonal Quota /ˈsiː.zən.əl ˈkwoʊ.tə/ Enforceable legal limit on tourist entries during peak summer months.
Short-Term Rental /ˌʃɔːt tɜːm ˈren.təl/ Furnished residential flats leased to tourists rather than locals. Heritage Conservation /ˈher.ɪ.tɪdʒ/ Preserving historical architecture, monuments, and indigenous ways of life.
